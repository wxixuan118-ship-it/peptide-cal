How the Peptide Blend Calculator Works

A blend vial holds two or more peptides as one powder. Once you add water, every peptide dissolves into the same liquid, so each has its own concentration but they always travel together. You cannot draw one without the others. That is why a peptide blend calculator asks you to pick a single component to measure by, then reports what the rest add up to.

mL per dose = dose of chosen peptide (mg) × water (mL) ÷ chosen peptide in vial (mg)
other peptide per dose = its mg in vial ÷ water (mL) × mL per dose

Units on a U-100 insulin syringe are the mL figure times 100. Doses per vial come from the chosen peptide alone, because every component runs out at the same moment.

Why a Blend Keeps Its Ratio

Adding more or less water changes how many units you draw, but never the ratio between peptides. A 10 mg + 5 mg vial always gives two parts of Peptide A to one part of Peptide B. If you need a different ratio, a blend cannot provide it; separate vials can.

Peptide Blend Calculator Example

Take the default inputs: a vial with 10 mg of Peptide A and 5 mg of Peptide B, reconstituted with 2 mL of bacteriostatic water, measured by a 500 mcg dose of Peptide A.

  • Peptide A concentration: 10 mg ÷ 2 mL = 5 mg/mL.
  • Volume per dose: 0.5 mg ÷ 5 mg/mL = 0.1 mL, which is 10 units.
  • Peptide B in that draw: 5 mg ÷ 2 mL × 0.1 mL = 0.25 mg (250 mcg).
  • Doses per vial: 10 mg ÷ 0.5 mg = 20, and Peptide B runs out at the same time.

Switch the calculator to measure by Peptide B at 250 mcg and the answer is still 10 units. Both inputs describe the same draw from the same vial.

Three-Peptide Blends

Some blends combine three peptides in very different amounts, for example 50 mg + 10 mg + 10 mg. This peptide calculator for blends handles them the same way: enter all three, choose the one your plan is written around, and read the other two from the table. With 3 mL of water and a 2 mg dose of the 50 mg component, you draw 12 units and receive 400 mcg of each 10 mg component.

The total blend strength (all peptides added together, per mL) is useful for one thing: checking the label. Many blend vials state only the total mg, so compare that figure against the sum of the components before you rely on the numbers.

Common Peptide Blend Mistakes

  • Using the total mg as one peptide. A 10 mg blend vial may hold 5 mg of each peptide. Entering 10 mg for the one you dose by doubles its assumed strength and halves the units you draw.
  • Dosing by the wrong component. Your plan names a dose for a specific peptide. Make sure the "measure by" selector matches it.
  • Expecting to adjust one peptide alone. Raising the units raises every component. Changing one peptide's dose without the others needs separate vials.
  • Mixing up mg and mcg. A 250 mcg dose is 0.25 mg; check the unit selector before you read the result.

Peptide Blend Calculator FAQ

How do you calculate the dose of a peptide blend?

Pick the peptide your plan is based on. Divide its dose by its concentration (its mg in the vial divided by the water) to get mL, then multiply by 100 for U-100 units. Every other peptide arrives in the same draw at the vial ratio.

Can I dose each peptide in a blend separately?

No. All peptides in a blend are dissolved in the same liquid, so any draw contains all of them in a fixed ratio. Separate doses require separate vials.

Does adding more water change the blend ratio?

No. Water changes every concentration by the same factor, so the ratio between peptides stays the same. Only the units you draw for a given dose change.

Which peptide should the blend calculator measure by?

Measure by the component your prescriber or protocol gives a dose for. If several doses are listed, check that the vial ratio actually matches them before mixing.

Is the peptide blend calculator free?

Yes. It is free, needs no account, and runs entirely in your browser, so nothing you enter is sent to a server.
