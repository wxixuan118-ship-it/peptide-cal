How to Use the Peptide Calculator

You need three numbers from your vial label and your plan. The tool handles the unit conversions, so you never have to move a decimal point by hand.

  1. Enter the vial amount. This is the total peptide printed on the label, usually in mg (for example 5 mg or 10 mg). Use the mcg option if the label lists micrograms.
  2. Enter the water you added. Type the millilitres of bacteriostatic water used to reconstitute the powder. More water means a weaker solution and more units per dose.
  3. Enter your dose. Type the amount prescribed or planned for one injection, in mcg or mg.
  4. Pick your syringe. The calculator warns you if the draw does not fit, or if it is too small to measure well.
  5. Read the result. The large number is how many units to pull on the syringe. The drawing shows roughly where the plunger should sit.

Have not mixed the vial yet? Plan the water first with the peptide reconstitution calculator, which works backwards from the units you want to draw.

Peptide Calculator Formula

The arithmetic takes three steps. Knowing it lets you double-check any result on paper.

1. Peptide concentration

concentration (mg/mL) = peptide in vial (mg) ÷ water added (mL)

2. Peptide volume per dose

volume (mL) = dose (mg) ÷ concentration (mg/mL)

3. Peptide dose in syringe units

units = volume (mL) × 100

The factor of 100 comes from the U-100 insulin syringe: one millilitre is divided into 100 units, so each unit holds 0.01 mL. It is the standard syringe for small subcutaneous volumes, and the one this page assumes.

Peptide Calculator Example: 5 mg Vial

Say you have a 5 mg vial, you add 2 mL of bacteriostatic water, and your dose is 250 mcg. Here is the working:

  • Concentration: 5 mg ÷ 2 mL = 2.5 mg/mL, which is 2,500 mcg per mL.
  • Volume: 0.25 mg ÷ 2.5 mg/mL = 0.10 mL.
  • Units: 0.10 mL × 100 = 10 units on a U-100 syringe.
  • Doses per vial: 5 mg ÷ 0.25 mg = 20 doses.

If you had added 1 mL instead, the same 250 mcg dose would be 5 units. Less water gives a stronger solution and a smaller draw. With 3 mL it would be 15 units. The peptide dose is identical in all three cases — only the volume changes.

Peptide Calculator Accuracy Tips

The math is exact, but the draw is only as good as the inputs. A few habits keep results meaningful in practice:

  • Aim for 5–50 units per dose. Draws under 2–3 units are hard to read. If your result is tiny, add more water when you next reconstitute.
  • Match the syringe to the draw. A 0.3 mL syringe has 1-unit lines, so small doses are easier to measure than on a 1 mL barrel.
  • Write the concentration on the vial. Once mixed, label the vial with mg/mL so you never have to remember how much water went in.
  • Re-check the label. Some vials state total content per vial and others per pack. Enter the amount in the vial you are actually using.

Peptide Calculator FAQ

How does a peptide calculator convert mg to units?

It divides the vial's mg by the water added to get mg per mL, divides your dose by that concentration to get mL, then multiplies by 100 because a U-100 syringe has 100 units per mL.

Is this peptide calculator free?

Yes. It is free, needs no account, and runs entirely in your browser, so the numbers you enter are never sent to a server.

Does more water change the peptide dose?

No. Water only changes the concentration. With more water you draw more units for the same peptide dose; with less water you draw fewer units.

Can the peptide calculator handle blends?

Yes, if you calculate one component at a time. Enter that component's mg in the vial and its dose; the units you get also deliver the other components in the same ratio as the blend.

Which syringe does the peptide calculator assume?

A U-100 insulin syringe, where 1 mL equals 100 units. If you use a different syringe, convert the mL result shown instead of the unit figure.

Is a peptides calculator the same as a dosage calculator?

They use the same formula. A peptides calculator usually focuses on units per injection, while a dosage calculator also adds frequency and vial supply.
