mg to mL Calculator

Convert mass to volume or volume to mass using the concentration of a solution. For a pure liquid, use its density instead. Enter your own known value: mg, g and mL cannot be converted directly without it.

Mass and volume conversion

Use concentration for a substance dissolved in a solution. Use density only for the mass of the liquid itself.

For example, 5 mg/mL means each 1 mL contains 5 mg of the substance.

Result 2 mL

10 mg ÷ 5 mg/mL = 2 mL

This is an arithmetic conversion. It does not determine a dose, suitable diluent, or product safety.

How to convert mg to mL

For a solution, divide the substance's mass in mg by its concentration in mg/mL. For example, 10 mg at 5 mg/mL occupies 2 mL of that solution.

volume (mL) = mass (mg) ÷ concentration (mg/mL)

How to convert mL to mg

Multiply the solution volume in mL by its concentration in mg/mL. For example, 2 mL at 5 mg/mL contains 10 mg of the dissolved substance.

mass (mg) = volume (mL) × concentration (mg/mL)

How to convert g to mL

For a pure liquid, divide its mass in grams by its density in g/mL. For a dissolved substance, convert grams to milligrams first (1 g = 1,000 mg), then divide by the solution concentration in mg/mL. These are different questions: density describes the liquid's own mass; concentration describes how much of a particular substance it contains.

pure liquid volume (mL) = mass (g) ÷ density (g/mL)

When the conversion is impossible

Mass and volume measure different things. A number such as “10 mg” alone does not tell you how many mL it represents. Find the product's stated concentration or a reliable density for the exact material before calculating. Do not assume every liquid has the density of water.

Frequently asked questions

Can I convert mg to mL without concentration?

No. You need the concentration of the substance in mg/mL, or a density when measuring the mass of a pure liquid.

How do I use mg/mL to find mL?

Divide the required mass in mg by the known concentration in mg/mL. The result is a volume in mL.

Is 1 g always 1 mL?

No. That would require a density of exactly 1 g/mL. Other liquids have different densities, and a dissolved substance requires its concentration instead.
